# Where to send the trade article

From the "Trade Article Platforms" doc (Ankita's), AI/ML tab, read 3 Sep 2026.
Only the outlets that actually fit a container/runtime security and performance
piece are listed. Word counts and rules are quoted from that doc.

## Fits our piece

| Outlet | Words | How | Contact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **VentureBeat** guest post | 800-1200 | Typeform, editable Google Doc. Full article only, no pitches or outlines. Response up to 14 business days. | https://venturebeat.com/guest-posts (no email; tips@venturebeat.com is news only) |
| **InformationWeek** (TechTarget) | <=850 | Full draft only, no pitches. Attach as Word doc. | informationweek@techtarget.com |
| **No Jitter** (TechTarget) | 750-1000 | Pitch first: story angle, why it matters, audience, your expertise, word count. | via contributor guidelines page |
| **IEEE Spectrum** | varies | IEEE Author Center | - |
| **MIT Technology Review** | varies | commissioned; opinion desk | opinion@technologyreview.com |

VentureBeat explicitly wants "actionable insights or technical deep dives on
AI/ML (e.g., LLMs, AI agents)", which is what this piece is.

InformationWeek also wants: headline <=60 chars, summary <=160 chars, byline,
bio under 100 words, photo 300x300 JPG, fact-checked and spell-checked.

No Jitter wants: headline <=57 chars plus a deck <=160 chars, AP style, active
voice, jargon-free, exclusive.

## Not in the doc but worth pitching

The New Stack and InfoQ are the natural editorial home for container runtime
content and neither appears in the doc. Pitch after the first placement lands.

## Two rules that bind us

1. **VentureBeat and No Jitter both ban AI-generated content outright.** No
   Jitter allows assistive tools like spell-check and nothing further. The
   article has to be written by the author. This is the same constraint as the
   paper's open retype item, and it is now an outlet rule, not only a personal
   standard.
2. **Exclusive.** VentureBeat, InformationWeek and No Jitter each want the
   piece to be theirs alone. Submit to one at a time. No simultaneous
   submissions.

## What this changes about the draft

The design was built for ~1500 words. VentureBeat caps at 1200 and
InformationWeek at 850. Target 1200 for VentureBeat first, and keep an 850-word
cut for InformationWeek if VentureBeat passes.

## What the doc says makes a trade article good

Written by professionals for professionals, published somewhere industry-specific.
Use field-standard terms and acronyms freely. Include real specs, thresholds or
benchmarks. Assume the reader wants the technical detail. Compare directly to
known standards or competitors. Practical: real-world problems, methodologies, tools.

Our piece already does all five: it uses runc, gVisor, Kata, Yama and cgroup
terms without apology, quotes measured thresholds, and compares directly against
raw Docker and OpenHands.

## Why it matters beyond publication

The doc's EB-1A tab lists the criteria a trade article can support:
authorship of scholarly articles (USCIS accepts industry-focused platforms, not
only academic journals), original contributions of major significance, published
material about the beneficiary, and leading or critical role via recurring
columns. Keep the publication details, editorial selection evidence, and
readership metrics for each placement.
